# Skill: onboarding a first-time co-owner

For [hr/hiring.md](../../../hr/hiring.md) when the human on the other
side has never hired an AI colleague before. The process itself doesn't
change — same 16 steps, same stop criterion, same probation. This is
about pacing and framing, so someone with zero context isn't just
technically walked through it but actually follows what's happening
and why.

## Before Phase 1
Say in one sentence what's about to happen and why, before question 1
lands: a personnel file instead of a one-shot prompt, an assessment
center before anyone goes live, a probation period with measurable
criteria — "like a human colleague, on purpose, not as a metaphor."
Skip this for someone who has clearly done this before (a returning
co-owner, a second hire in the same sitting) — saying it twice reads as
not having listened the first time.

## During Phases 1–3
- The stop criterion (question 2–3) is the one place a first-timer
  tends to push back — "but I *do* need this occasionally." Don't
  soften it and don't relitigate it endlessly either: state the
  one-off/recurring distinction once, plainly, and let them decide with
  it in view.
- After Phase 2 (the personnel questions), before moving into naming —
  this is the natural pause the check-in habit in your own profile is
  for: "that's the substance settled — want to see candidate
  personalities next, or do you want to sit with the access rights
  question a bit longer first?" Not a formality; if they're still
  turning something over, staying there is the right call.
- Three candidate personalities (Phase 3) can overwhelm someone who
  doesn't yet have a feel for what a personality even changes in
  practice. If they seem to be picking on name alone, ask what tone
  they'd want the colleague to push back in — that's usually enough to
  make the three options concrete instead of decorative.

## Explaining without over-explaining
A first-timer not knowing a term ("what's an adapter?") is a request
for a definition, not an invitation to explain the whole architecture.
Answer the specific question, then stop — more detail than was asked
for reads as doubting they can follow along, which is exactly the
patronizing failure mode your own profile already rules out.

## After Phase 4 closes
Once the personnel file, memory, journal, skills folder, and adapter
exist: say plainly what happens next (assessment center, then
probation) and ask the concrete version of the check-in — not "need
anything else?" but "want to sit in on the assessment center for this
one, or are you comfortable if I run it and bring you the report?"
Their answer is itself useful signal for how much pacing the *next*
hire will need.
